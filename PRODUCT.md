# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hiring managers, ML researchers, senior engineers, and potential collaborators who are evaluating technical candidates. They arrive from LinkedIn, GitHub, or a referral. They're skimming fast — portfolio, resume, and a handful of projects — deciding in under two minutes whether to read further or reach out. Their question is not "can this person code?" but "is this person someone exceptional I want to work with?"

## Product Purpose

Carl Emmanuel Macabales is a CS student specializing in AI/ML with a strong software engineering foundation. The portfolio exists to make that depth legible at a glance — not by listing skills, but by demonstrating the quality of thinking and craft behind the work. Success means a visitor leaves with a specific, vivid sense of who Cem is and what he's capable of, rather than a vague impression of "another dev with a portfolio."

## Brand Personality

Curious, creative, driven. The portfolio should feel like a person who is genuinely excited about what they're building — not someone performing competence for an audience. Confident without being loud. Exploratory without being chaotic.

## Anti-references

- **Dark + neon-teal card grids**: The current aesthetic direction — the saturated AI-developer-portfolio template (black bg, teal accent, identical spotlight cards in a grid). This is what the portfolio currently looks like and needs to break out of.
- **Cream / warm minimal**: Sand-colored Notion-aesthetic, typographic-only, 2026 AI default. Too safe, reads as a designer not an engineer.
- **Corporate SaaS**: Navy/gray palette, metric cards, "hero-metric template" energy. Looks like a product landing page, not a person.
- **Hacker terminal**: Green-on-black, monospace everywhere, typewriter-only aesthetic. Overdone cliché; signals style over substance.

## Design Principles

1. **Depth signals, not breadth lists**: Every section should demonstrate how Cem thinks, not just what he knows. Projects lead with insight, not just tech stack.
2. **Distinctly personal**: The visual identity should be unmistakably Cem's — if someone could template-match it to a category ("the ML dev portfolio"), it hasn't worked.
3. **Curiosity as the motion principle**: Interactivity and animation should feel like exploration — things that reward attention rather than play on loop. Motion earns its place by revealing something.
4. **Memorability over safety**: A visitor who forgets the portfolio immediately is a failed visit. One unexpected visual or structural choice that sticks is worth more than a conventionally well-executed page.
5. **Show, don't label**: The copy explains what Cem built and why it matters, not adjectives about Cem himself. Let the work carry the personality.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AAA target. All text at ≥7:1 contrast against its background. Full keyboard navigation. Reduced-motion alternatives for every animation. Color is never the sole information carrier.
